# Afrodescendant Ali Visual Style Template v0.1

Status: REFERENCE — PRE-PRODUCTION / UNPUBLISHED  
Project: PM-009 — AfrodescendantAli.com  
Use: Future article, analysis, explainer, case-file, community-report and culture packages

## 1. House visual direction

### Name

**Signal Editorial Illustration**

### Purpose

Give Afrodescendant Ali writing a consistent visual identity across the Black World while preserving regional specificity, factual restraint, accessibility and image rights.

The visual should feel:

- editorial, not promotional;
- human, not sensational;
- contemporary, not generic;
- Pan-African and diasporic, without flattening distinct places;
- visually confident, but honest about what is illustration and what is evidence.

## 2. Core style tokens

| Token | Default treatment |
| --- | --- |
| Format | Wide landscape hero, designed for 16:9 and safe mobile crops |
| Composition | Subject-led scene with a clear focal point and usable negative space for headline overlays |
| Medium | Painterly editorial realism or high-quality commissioned illustration; not a copied photograph |
| Light | Natural daylight, warm late-afternoon or clear morning light; use storm light only when relevant |
| Mood | Cautious hope, civic attention, dignity, continuity and measured urgency |
| Palette | Charcoal, cream, sand, terracotta, forest green, muted teal and warm gold |
| Typography inside image | None by default; headlines and labels belong to the interface |
| Logos and flags | None unless separately verified and cleared |
| People | Use non-identifiable subjects for generated illustrations; protect minors and vulnerable people |
| Crop | Preserve faces, location cues and factual context across desktop, card and mobile variants |

The developing-story red is a site-interface signal. It should not be forced into every image.

## 3. Image-source hierarchy

Choose the source before choosing the treatment.

1. Afrodescendant Ali original or commissioned image.
2. Contributor or community image with consent and safety review.
3. Licensed editorial or stock image with recorded terms.
4. Official institutional image where the planned use is permitted.
5. Verified public-domain image.
6. Commissioned or generated illustration, clearly labeled.
7. Typographic, map, chart or document-led fallback.

RSS and publisher images remain discovery-only unless separately cleared. A source thumbnail is never a rights record.

## 4. Visual treatment by article type

| Article type | Preferred treatment | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| News brief | Cleared scene, official document, licensed image or restrained fallback | Using generated art as evidence of a reported event |
| Analysis | Signal editorial illustration, map, chart or conceptual scene | Presenting interpretation as a literal photograph |
| Explainer | Diagram, map, timeline, document detail or labeled illustration | Decorative imagery that obscures the explanation |
| Timeline | Rights-cleared archive image or timeline graphic | Undated archive imagery presented as current |
| Case file | Court/public record, location, document or restrained graphic | Crime-scene spectacle, identifiable vulnerable people |
| Community report | Consented local image, contributor image or community-led illustration | Extractive or anonymous “poverty” imagery |
| Culture/media | Artist-approved still, artwork, portrait or licensed image | Unlicensed screenshots, posters or performance stills |
| Editorial | Commissioned illustration, portrait with permission or typographic treatment | Manufactured documentary authority |

## 5. Lane adaptation

The house style stays consistent while the visual context changes.

- **Africa:** show the specific city, landscape, institution, corridor or community. Avoid generic “African” scenery.
- **Black America:** use recognizable civic, neighborhood, workplace, cultural or institutional settings. Avoid reducing stories to protest imagery.
- **Caribbean:** preserve island architecture, coast, weather, agriculture, school, community and urban context without resorting to postcard imagery.
- **Afro-Latin America:** identify the country, language, city or community where known. Avoid blending multiple national identities into one scene.
- **Afro-Europe:** show the actual city, migration corridor, institution, workplace or cultural setting. Avoid treating Europe as visually neutral.
- **Indian Ocean and global corridors:** use maritime, island, diaspora, trade, migration or cultural cues only when supported by the story.

Regional cues must come from the reporting, not from assumptions added to the prompt.

## 6. Reusable generation brief

Use this brief when a cleared photograph, contributor image or institutional asset is unavailable and an illustration is editorially appropriate.

    Use case: illustration-story | stylized-concept | infographic-diagram
    Asset type: article hero | article card | explainer visual | case-file graphic
    Primary request: one sentence describing the story's visual function
    Region/lane: specific country, city, community or corridor
    Scene/backdrop: verified setting; do not invent a named location
    Subject: people, place, document, object or system
    Style/medium: Signal editorial illustration; painterly realism; clearly illustrative, not documentary photography
    Composition/framing: wide landscape; focal subject on one side; negative space for headline; safe mobile crop
    Lighting/mood: natural light and editorial mood appropriate to the story
    Color palette: charcoal, cream, sand, terracotta, forest green, muted teal and warm gold
    Text in image: none
    Constraints: no logos, no watermark, no copied source image, no identifiable real person, no fabricated event details
    Avoid: stereotypes, sensational damage, generic continental imagery, visual claims not supported by the article
    Label: Illustration — not documentary photography

Do not add real names, exact buildings, logos, flags, dates, uniforms or event-specific details unless the article's evidence and the asset rights support them.

## 7. Asset record required for every writing

| Field | Required entry |
| --- | --- |
| Asset ID | Stable ID such as IMG-YYYYMMDD-### |
| Article ID | Linked article/sample |
| Visual status | IMAGE_NEEDED, IMAGE_DISCOVERY, IMAGE_RIGHTS_REVIEW, IMAGE_READY, IMAGE_FALLBACK_ONLY, IMAGE_HOLD, IMAGE_REJECTED or IMAGE_PUBLISHED |
| Asset type | Photo, video frame, illustration, map, chart or fallback card |
| Source/creator | Person, institution, license, contributor or generator |
| Original URL/delivery record | Direct provenance |
| Rights state | cleared_original, contributor_consent, licensed, public_domain, permission_pending, discovery_only, fallback_only or rejected |
| Use note | Documentary, illustrative, conceptual or fallback purpose |
| Credit line | Exact displayed credit |
| Caption | Fact-checked description |
| Alt text | Concise factual accessibility description |
| Crop variants | Hero, card, mobile and social as required |
| Review | Rights, factual fit, safety, accessibility and mobile checks |

Generated visuals should normally use fallback_only and must state that they are illustrations rather than documentary evidence.

## 8. Editorial image checklist

Before an article can move to editorial approval:

- [ ] Image function is defined.
- [ ] Visual source was selected through the rights hierarchy.
- [ ] No RSS or publisher image was reused without clearance.
- [ ] Image does not imply a fact the article has not established.
- [ ] Caption, credit and alt text are complete.
- [ ] Generated or illustrative visuals are labeled.
- [ ] No identifiable real person is fabricated or misrepresented.
- [ ] Sensitive subjects, minors and vulnerable communities received extra review.
- [ ] Desktop, card and mobile crops preserve meaning.
- [ ] Broken-image and withdrawal fallbacks are tested.
- [ ] Asset record is linked to the article.
- [ ] Editor approval is recorded.

## 9. Reference applications

The first three sample applications establish the baseline:

- Dakar Youth Olympics: participation, public space, inclusion and continental significance.
- Jamaica roof recovery: housing repair, community verification and cautious recovery.
- Jamaica school recovery: continuity, repaired infrastructure and return to learning.

These are conceptual illustrations. They establish the visual language, not documentary evidence of the events.

## 10. Release boundary

This template authorizes reuse of the visual system, not automatic image creation or publication. Every future article still requires a separate source, rights, image, claim, editorial and release decision.
