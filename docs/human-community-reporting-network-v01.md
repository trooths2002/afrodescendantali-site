# Human and Community Reporting Network v0.1

Date: 2026-10-28
Project: PM-009 — AfrodescendantAli.com
Status: internal intake framework; no recruitment or outreach sent

## Purpose

Create one accountable human reporting channel for each Black World lane so the site is not dependent on RSS or external aggregation.

## Six reporting desks

| Desk | Initial scope | Preferred contributor profile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Africa | Regional politics, trade, climate, public health, culture and diaspora links | Local journalist, researcher or trusted community reporter with country-level access |
| Black America | Policy, civic life, labor, culture, education, justice and local community change | Black newsroom contributor, civic reporter or community journalist |
| Caribbean | Island and mainland Caribbean affairs, climate, migration, trade, culture and disaster response | Contributor with island-specific knowledge and regional contacts |
| Afro-Latin America | Afro-descendant rights, politics, culture, migration and community life in Spanish- or Portuguese-speaking countries | Bilingual or multilingual correspondent with local reporting ability |
| Afro-Europe | Black European communities, migration, policy, memory, culture and representation | Country-based diaspora reporter or researcher |
| Indian Ocean and global corridors | Indian Ocean islands, diaspora mobility, trade, migration and cross-border identity | Regional correspondent or diaspora-network reporter |

## Intake record

Every prospective contributor record should include:

- name or professional identifier;
- country, city and coverage geography;
- languages;
- beat and subject expertise;
- links to prior work or a sample assignment;
- conflicts, affiliations and relevant relationships;
- preferred communication channel;
- consent to retain and use submitted material;
- confirmation of original work or permission to submit;
- safety or anonymity needs;
- editor assessment and decision.

## Minimum acceptance standard

A contributor may enter a supervised trial only after:

1. Identity or professional history is reasonably verified.
2. Geography and beat are clear.
3. Conflicts and affiliations are disclosed.
4. Rights to submitted material are documented.
5. The contributor accepts attribution and correction procedures.
6. A short trial assignment is reviewed against the source and claim standard.

## Trial assignment

The first assignment should be a short source-grounded brief, not a breaking-news claim. It must include:

- one primary source;
- one local or directly observed source where available;
- a source list;
- what is confirmed;
- what remains unclear;
- the proposed headline and attribution;
- a direct link to the source material.

The editor decides whether the output is:

- accepted for publication;
- returned for clarification;
- held for corroboration;
- rejected for rights, sourcing or accuracy reasons.

## Editorial and safety rules

- No anonymous allegation is published without a documented verification path.
- Do not publish personal data that is not necessary to the story.
- Distinguish reporting, official statements, analysis and community testimony.
- Preserve uncertainty instead of filling gaps with inference.
- Corrections and takedown requests require an auditable record.
- Contributors do not receive authority to publish directly to production.

## Network formation sequence

1. Nominate one internal owner for each desk.
2. Build a shortlist of three prospective contributors or partner organizations per desk.
3. Review identity, geography, beat, prior work, conflicts and rights.
4. Run one supervised trial assignment.
5. Record the decision in the contributor ledger.
6. Promote only approved contributors to the editorial queue.

No recruitment message, contributor agreement or payment commitment is created by this document. Those require separate owner approval.
