# Prospective buyer validation guide

Internal use only. This guide tests whether prospective buyers understand the three public offers and see a reason to pay. It does not authorize outreach, named-client proof, pricing publication, or production changes.

## Opening prompt

“I am testing how clearly this practice explains its work. Please read the three descriptions as if you were looking for help. Which one sounds closest to a problem you have now, if any?”

Do not explain the terms before the buyer chooses. Record their words first.

## Test sequence

1. Show only the homepage headline and three problem routes.
2. Ask which route they would choose and why.
3. Ask what they think they would receive.
4. Ask what they would need to provide to begin.
5. Ask what event, deadline, risk, launch, funder, audience, or decision would make the work urgent.
6. Ask what would make the work worth paying for.
7. Show the detailed services page and ask what remains unclear.
8. Ask whether the wording “Black World” helps them recognize fit and what they understand it to mean.

## Message-pass criteria

Treat the message as working only when buyers can choose a route without asking what the service name means, describe a plausible deliverable, identify what they would bring, name a real urgency trigger, explain why the result could be worth paying for, and distinguish Ali's public work from AGV's organizational systems and commercial work.

## Evidence discipline

Public procurement and program documents establish that these demand situations exist. They do not establish that a particular buyer will select Ali, accept a price, or approve a case study. Keep buyer language, pricing feedback, delivery hours, close rates, proof permissions, and public evidence in separate records.
