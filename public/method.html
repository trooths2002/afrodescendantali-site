Method

From public signal to a responsible public record

The desk reads public signals through power, capital, routes, media, and historical memory. It separates discovery from publication, then routes verified work toward a public brief, analysis, briefing, memo, or system audit.

Operating process

The daily desk rhythm

  1. Discover. Review approved primary institutions, accountable local publishers, official data, and trusted reporting for signals across the Black World.
  2. Scope. Record the event, people affected, geography, date, source type, rights boundary, and why the signal matters.
  3. Verify. Check the primary record, corroborate consequential claims, seek relevant responses, and preserve direct links.
  4. Label. Distinguish a news brief, source note, analysis, commentary, live update, sponsored item, and unknown.
  5. Publish or hold. Publish only when evidence and rights support it; otherwise keep the item in the private review queue or do not publish.
Source discipline

Primary records: official statements, public filings, court records, public data, and direct interviews are preferred.

Independent reporting: local and specialist reporting helps establish context; circular repetition is not corroboration.

Feeds and social posts: discovery inputs only until the underlying claim and source are checked.

Public record: each published item shows its type, date, author or desk, direct sources, method, and what remains unknown.

Daily review: automated discovery can create a review artifact, but it cannot publish a claim or change the public site.
