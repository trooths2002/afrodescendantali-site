The delivery loop

Ten stages from messy input to reusable capability.

The level of formality changes with the project, but these questions keep complex work grounded.

  1. 01 / Intake

    What is the real job?

    Capture objective, deadline, owner, existing material and what would make the work useful.

  2. 02 / Source truth

    What actually controls?

    Separate primary records, authoritative sources, client instructions, drafts and memory.

  3. 03 / Scope

    What belongs in this run?

    Define deliverable, audience, exclusions, rights, commercial terms and approval boundaries.

  4. 04 / Evidence

    What can be proved?

    Verify claims and state, search for contradictions and preserve uncertainty.

  5. 05 / Build

    What makes the evidence useful?

    Turn sources into the needed media, page, brief, chronology, dashboard or operating structure.

  6. 06 / QA

    Does it hold up?

    Check names, dates, links, claims, rights, routes, build behavior and high-risk failure points.

  7. 07 / Approval

    Who has authority now?

    Keep readiness separate from permission to publish, deploy, spend or represent someone publicly.

  8. 08 / Delivery

    What was actually delivered?

    Preserve the deliverable, receipt, limitations and next action outside chat memory.

  9. 09 / Learning

    What should improve?

    Capture corrections, recurring bottlenecks, feedback and evidence-backed lessons.

  10. 10 / Capability

    What can now be repeated?

    Extract the reusable skill, workflow, deliverable and public-safe proof the work earned.

Black World editorial method

The intelligence discipline remains a distinct layer.

Public analysis still follows the desk logic: observe the signal, establish what is confirmed, locate the system underneath, interpret the Black World consequence and identify what to watch next.

That editorial method builds authority. The commercial delivery loop above governs client work. One does not need to masquerade as the other.
