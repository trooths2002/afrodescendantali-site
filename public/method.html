How I Work

The work changes. The control loop stays recognizable.

Research, media, archives, websites, and institutional systems look different on the surface. The underlying delivery process is consistent: establish source truth, make uncertainty visible, build against the real job, test the result, preserve approval boundaries, and turn completed work into reusable knowledge.

The delivery loop

Ten stages from messy input to reusable capability.

Not every project needs the same amount of ceremony, but these are the recurring questions that keep complex work grounded.

  1. 01 / Intake

    What is the real job?

    Capture the request, deadline, owner, material already available, and what would make the work useful.

  2. 02 / Source truth

    What actually controls?

    Separate primary records, authoritative sources, client instructions, drafts, and memory before building on them.

  3. 03 / Scope

    What belongs in this run?

    Define the bounded problem, deliverable, audience, exclusions, and any approval or rights boundaries.

  4. 04 / Evidence

    What can be proved?

    Verify claims and state, search for contradictions, classify uncertainty, and preserve safe wording.

  5. 05 / Build

    What makes the evidence usable?

    Turn the source material into the needed media, page, chronology, dashboard, memo, or operating structure.

  6. 06 / QA

    Does the result hold up?

    Check names, dates, links, claims, state, rights, routes, build behavior, and the parts most likely to fail.

  7. 07 / Approval

    Who has authority now?

    Keep draft readiness separate from permission to publish, deploy, spend, commit, or represent someone publicly.

  8. 08 / Delivery

    What was actually delivered?

    Preserve the final deliverable, receipt, open limitations, and the next action instead of relying on chat memory.

  9. 09 / Learning

    What should improve next time?

    Capture corrections, defects, recurring bottlenecks, useful client feedback, and evidence-backed lessons.

  10. 10 / Capability

    What can now be repeated?

    Extract the reusable knowledge, skill, workflow, deliverable, and public-safe proof the project has earned.

Evidence boundary

A correct result is sometimes a HOLD.

If a statistic cannot be reproduced, a credential cannot be verified, a date conflicts, a client approval is missing, or the live infrastructure cannot be read back, the workflow should say so. Uncertainty is not removed just because a deadline exists.

That creates a useful client-facing outcome even before everything is resolved: what is known, what was built, what remains open, what evidence would close it, and what can safely happen next.

From work to capability

The goal is not to repeat the project. It is to retain the method.

When a process shows up across different engagements, it becomes a stronger capability: research and verification, archive intelligence, launch readiness, institutional websites, knowledge systems, operating dashboards, or AI-assisted workflow design.
