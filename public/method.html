Method

How we turn a signal into a story

We start with what people need to know: what happened, who is affected, what is documented, and what remains unclear. Then we choose the right format — news, analysis, briefing, or research.

Our process

Every story follows the same path

  1. Find. Review approved primary institutions, accountable local publishers, official data, and trusted reporting for signals across the Black World.
  2. Check. Record the event, people affected, geography, date, source type, rights boundary, and why the signal matters.
  3. Explain. Check the primary record, corroborate consequential claims, seek relevant responses, and preserve direct links.
  4. Label. Distinguish a news brief, source note, analysis, commentary, live update, sponsored item, and unknown.
  5. Publish when ready. Publish only when evidence and rights support it; otherwise keep the item in the private review queue or do not publish.
What we check

Start with the source: official statements, public filings, court records, public data, and direct interviews are preferred.

Add accountable reporting: local and specialist reporting helps establish context; circular repetition is not corroboration.

Treat leads as leads: discovery inputs only until the underlying claim and source are checked.

Show the reader: each published item shows its type, date, author or desk, direct sources, method, and what remains unknown.

Keep a human editor in the loop: automated discovery can create a review artifact, but it cannot publish a claim or change the public site.
