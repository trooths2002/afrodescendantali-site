Ninth Circuit rejects DOJ bid to block more than 20,000 California ballots

The latest order keeps the dispute inside ordinary judicial safeguards as voting-rights groups challenge federal demands aimed at California election officials.

30 October 2026Updated 31 October 2026Afrodescendant Ali News Desk

What happened

The NAACP said the U.S. Court of Appeals for the Ninth Circuit rejected, for a second time, a Department of Justice attempt to force California to block the counting of ballots connected to a federal voter list. The NAACP said the dispute concerns more than 20,000 Californians.

Campaign Legal Center's case page lists an October 30 Ninth Circuit order denying an injunction pending appeal. The same page lists the district-court order and the intervention filings by the NAACP and LULAC.

What the legal record shows

The Justice Department says it identified people it believed were ineligible to vote and sued California after state officials declined to segregate the ballots. The NAACP and allied groups dispute the reliability and process behind that effort. Those are competing positions, not a final finding that every allegation is true.

What remains unknown

The litigation is ongoing. The October 30 order is not a final resolution of every underlying voter-list question, and the outcome of later proceedings remains open.

Reporting note

This brief distinguishes the NAACP and DOJ positions from the court orders. It does not characterize any voter as ineligible or guilty of wrongdoing.
