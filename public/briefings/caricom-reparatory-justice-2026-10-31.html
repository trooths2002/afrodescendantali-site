CARICOM says a British apology must be matched by repair

The regional body welcomed a possible formal apology from Britain while insisting that reparatory justice, debt, health, education, and restitution remain part of the conversation.

31 October 2026Afrodescendant Ali News Desk4 min read

What happened

In a statement issued on 31 October, CARICOM Chair Philip J. Pierre said the Community welcomed British Prime Minister Andy Burnham's expression of sorrow and regret and his openness to a formal apology and structured dialogue.

Pierre also said CARICOM does not accept the position that reparations are “not the right answer.” The statement frames reparatory justice as a question of responsibility held by states and institutions, not a claim of personal guilt against people living today.

What CARICOM is asking for

The statement says any dialogue should keep the CARICOM Ten-Point Plan for Reparatory Justice on the table. The plan includes a formal apology, public health, education and training, cultural restitution and reconnection, psychological rehabilitation, technology and entrepreneurship, debt cancellation, monetary compensation, and decolonisation.

CARICOM also said it would examine a British proposal to restructure the debt of the poorest Commonwealth countries, while warning that debt relief cannot substitute for reparatory justice or ignore the vulnerability of Caribbean states.

What remains unknown

The statement does not establish the terms, timetable, or outcome of a formal dialogue. It also does not show that Britain has accepted CARICOM's full reparations framework. Those questions remain open.

Reporting note

This brief is based on the official CARICOM statement. Historical and institutional characterizations are attributed to that statement; Afrodescendant Ali has not independently adjudicated every claim within it.
