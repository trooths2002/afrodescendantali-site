Corrections

If we get something wrong

If a fact, date, source link, image, or attribution is wrong, tell us. We investigate credible requests, make material changes clearly, and show readers what changed.

Public policy · version 0.19 November 2026

What you can flag

Tell us about a factual error, misleading attribution, incorrect date or location, broken source link, rights concern, privacy risk, or material omission. Please include the page URL, the specific passage, the evidence supporting the correction, and a way to follow up.

Email a correction request →

What happens next

  • Correction: a published fact, attribution, date, image, or link was materially wrong. We correct the page and identify the change.
  • Update: new verified information changes the state of an ongoing story. We add an update date and explain what is new.
  • Clarification: the underlying fact was substantially right, but wording could mislead. We revise the wording and explain the clarification when material.
  • Removal or privacy request: handled separately when safety, legal process, or a person’s privacy is at stake. A request is not an automatic takedown.

How we review it

  1. Log the request and preserve the original page state for review.
  2. Check the cited evidence against the source trail and any relevant response.
  3. Decide whether the issue is a correction, update, clarification, rights concern, or no change.
  4. For a material change, amend the page, update the visible date or correction note, and record what changed.
  5. Read the amended page back, including links and structured dates, before closing the correction.

What we will not do

We do not silently change material facts, remove a source trail to hide an error, or treat a disagreement with analysis as proof of inaccuracy. We also do not publish a correction until the evidence has been checked; a request can remain under review while that work happens.

Send only what we need

If a report concerns immediate danger, private personal information, or a rights-sensitive image, say so clearly in the subject line. Do not send passwords, financial information, or unnecessary personal data.
