How we report

How we report the news

Afrodescendant Ali is an independent Black World newsroom and research desk. Here is how we choose stories, check sources, label our work, and correct the record.

Public standard · version 0.19 November 2026

Know what you’re reading

  • News brief: original newsroom synthesis of a defined event or record. Publication date and source date are shown separately when they differ.
  • Source note: a direct, attributed link to a public source. It is not presented as independent reporting.
  • Analysis or explainer: interpretation built from identified evidence, with limits and open questions visible.
  • Commentary: an argument or perspective, clearly separated from reported news.
  • Sponsored or paid work: disclosed and kept separate from editorial judgment and news selection.
  • Live update: used only when the desk is maintaining an ongoing, time-stamped record. A static article is not labelled live.

Where our information comes from

We prefer the source closest to the event or decision: primary records, official statements, official data, direct interviews, and accountable local reporting. Secondary reporting helps locate leads and context; it does not automatically become proof. Feeds, social posts, tips, and AI outputs are discovery inputs until a human verifies the underlying record.

Before a story goes live

  1. Define the claim, event, geography, date, and people affected.
  2. Check the primary record and preserve a direct source link wherever possible.
  3. Corroborate consequential claims and seek a relevant response when someone is portrayed negatively.
  4. Separate confirmed facts, attributed claims, interpretation, and what remains unknown.
  5. Check rights, privacy, safety, conflict, and potential harm before using material or visuals.
  6. Show the author or desk, publication date, update date when applicable, and the method or source trail.

We review every day; we publish with care

We check approved sources every day. Automated discovery may help us find a lead, but it cannot approve a source, turn a headline into a claim, change a public page, or publish a story. A person makes the editorial decision. If the evidence is not strong enough, we do not publish.

Editorial work stays independent

Paid briefings, monitoring, research, and partnerships are separate products. A client, sponsor, buyer, or partner cannot approve, suppress, or rewrite editorial coverage. Conflicts, material relationships, and sponsored content should be disclosed when they could affect a reader’s understanding.

How we use tools and visuals

AI may assist with discovery, transcription, organization, or formatting. It is not a source, witness, or substitute for verification. We do not publish fabricated quotes, invented sources, or synthetic documentary evidence. Generated or illustrative visuals are labelled; third-party images, video, audio, and excerpts require a documented rights basis and appropriate credit.

How we make pages usable

Reader-facing pages should work with a keyboard, meaningful headings, skip links, text alternatives, readable contrast, responsive layouts, and motion that does not carry essential meaning. We use WCAG 2.2 as the accessibility target and test the rendered routes rather than treating a source-only check as enough.

Article pages use explicit publication and modification dates, canonical URLs, and structured data where appropriate. A daily review does not justify changing a date, sitemap, or indexability signal unless the underlying public record changed.

Questions and corrections

Questions, source concerns, and corrections are welcome. See the corrections policy or contact the desk. This page explains how we work; it is not a claim of certification.

Our review draws on public newsroom practices including the Trust Project indicators, the Journalism Trust Initiative standard, and the Associated Press News Values and Principles. Accessibility and publishing metadata are informed by WCAG 2.2 and Google’s Article structured-data guidance.
